Mall Visit App

A native app that turns a mall trip from a guessing game into a planned, seamless experience. Visit scheduling, accessibility booking, store discovery, dining, and events, all in one place.

Role
Product UX Designer
Year
2022
Platform
iOS · Android
Team
3 Designers · Product Owner · Mobile Developers · QA
The Challenge

A mall visit shouldn't require three apps and a prayer.

Malls are complex ecosystems, dozens of stores, restaurants, cinemas, and events, all happening at once. But the visitor experience was fractured. Finding out what was open, booking a table, or knowing whether a wheelchair would be available when you arrived meant navigating multiple siloed systems, or not knowing at all.

01
No planning tools
Visitors had no way to schedule a trip in advance. Wasted journeys, crowded arrivals, and no sense of what to expect on any given day.
02
Accessibility gaps
Visitors requiring a wheelchair, stroller, or other equipment had no way to check availability or pre-book before arriving, a significant barrier for families and people with accessibility needs.
03
Fragmented experience
Stores, dining, movies, and events all lived in different places, or nowhere digital at all. There was no single surface to discover, plan, and act.

And then pile on 5+ feature areas inside one native app. Keeping it cohesive became the real design challenge, not the features themselves, but making them feel like one product.

Process

Joining an existing product. Growing it beyond MVP.

When I joined, the app was already deployed in Europe, but at a very nascent, MVP stage. My job was to help build on it, under the guidance of senior designers from the client side.

01
Understanding the MVP
The MVP had skeleton screens across most flows. The navigation structure and overall IA were in place, but the individual journeys were incomplete stubs. Visit Planning, Accessibility Booking, Store Directory, and Table Booking all existed as entry points but had no designed flow behind them. I mapped each against the existing system before touching a single frame, understanding what the senior designers had established and where the gaps actually were.
02
Wireframes
New flows were validated at wireframe stage first, testing navigation and key interactions before committing to hi-fi. Sharing these with the team and client ensured alignment before visual work began.
03
Hi-fi Screens
Once wireframes were signed off, screens were built out at high fidelity. Where new patterns were needed that didn't exist in the design system yet, those were identified and flagged or contributed.

Design System

Contributing to a living design system.

The client had an existing design system. My relationship with it was practical: while building out flows, whenever something didn't exist or wasn't working for a new pattern, I'd flag it. Sometimes I'd add to the system directly. Other times it would go to the dedicated designer who owned the DS. It was an active, collaborative thing, not a document you consulted once.

Booking confirmation states
A consistent pattern for confirming visits, accessibility bookings, and table reservations, so users always knew exactly where they stood.
Availability indicators
Components to surface live availability for equipment and dining, open, limited, unavailable, without cluttering the interface.
Accessibility iconography
New icons for accessibility equipment categories, clear and neutral in tone, consistent with the client's existing visual language.
Event and offer cards
A flexible card pattern for mall events, promotions, and offers, scannable at a glance and expandable for more detail.
Outcomes

A product that grew past its MVP.

When I was part of the team, the app was deployed in Europe and actively growing. Four flows defined the scope of what I owned. Accessibility Booking and Table Booking were designed from scratch, no existing screens to build from. Visit Planning and Store Directory extended what was already there, the entry points existed in the MVP but the flows behind them were incomplete stubs that needed designing end to end. All four went from wireframe to signed-off hi-fi.

Learnings

What this project changed.

What working with structure taught me
This was my first time working with a highly structured client. The way they approached a problem, the rigour at each phase, how deliberately they moved from brief to execution, it changed how I think about process. Structure is not a constraint. It is what makes delivery reliable.
How significant design really is
This project made me understand, concretely, what design actually does. Not polish, not aesthetics, but the thing that makes a complex product usable or unusable. Five feature areas in one app, and design is what determines whether it feels like one thing or five. That realisation has shaped every project since.
