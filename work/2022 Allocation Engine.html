Allocation Engine

A two-week proof of concept to validate how rule-based automation could replace manual loan allocation, reducing operational overhead and improving decision consistency.

Role
Product UX Designer
Duration
2022
Industry
Data Engineering
Team
1 Product Manager · Stakeholder
Platform
Web
Proof of Concept
The Brief

Manual allocation was slowing everything down.

The current allocation process was hindered by manual, time-consuming tasks and a lack of centralised oversight. Every rule had to be configured by hand, re-allocations required intervention, and there was no single place to track performance across the system.

The Allocation Engine Management Portal was envisioned to fix this: a centralised platform covering rule configuration, re-allocation, attribute management, and performance reporting. This was a two-week POC to validate the concept before committing to full development.

Automate the allocation process through intuitive rule configuration interfaces.
02

03
Improve
Optimise resource utilisation through efficient re-allocation rules and controls.
04

Bring transparency and accountability through centralised management and reporting.
Understanding the Domain

Before designing, we had to understand what we were designing.

Before any wireframe was drawn, the first couple of days were spent understanding how rule engines are architected and what the core components needed to be.

The User

Emily spends her day monitoring case queues, adjusting allocation rules, generating reports, and analysing performance data. She needs a tool that helps her make data-driven decisions, improve efficiency, and reduce manual tasks.

Process

Five steps. Two weeks.

The two-week POC ran through five structured steps, from domain understanding to dev handover. With no visual design phase, the focus was entirely on validating the logic and flows through wireframes.

01
Understanding
Deep-dived into the subject matter through sessions with the PM and a domain expert. Understood how rule engines work, how allocation decisions are made, and what the system needed to do at its core.
02
Research
Researched existing rule and allocation engine implementations to understand established design patterns, configuration flows, and reporting structures used in similar systems.
03
Brainstorming
Ran collaborative sessions with the tech team and PM to map out flows and create wireframes. This was the core output of the POC: validating that the concept was logically sound before any visual design began.
04
Review
Presented wireframes to the client stakeholder for review and feedback. Iterated on the rule configuration and re-allocation flows based on their input before finalising the POC output.
05
Dev Handover
Handed the validated wireframes and annotated flows to the development team. The POC successfully proved the concept was buildable, paving the way for full visual design and development in the next phase.
Information Architecture

Mapping the structure before touching the screens.

The IA placed the Allocation Engine at the centre, branching into five core modules: Rule Management, Case Re-allocation, Attribute Management, Performance Reporting, and Attribute Metadata. This became the backbone for all wireframe decisions.

Wireframes

No visuals. Just validated flows.

The wireframes focused on the Rule Management module: listing existing rules, creating new rules, configuring attributes and conditions, and assigning actions. These were the core flows needed to prove the concept worked end-to-end.

Learnings

What a two-week POC teaches you

Domain first, design second
You cannot design what you do not understand. Investing the first couple of days in understanding rule engines meant every wireframe decision was grounded in how the system actually works, not assumptions.
Wireframes are a validation tool, not a deliverable
In a POC, the wireframe is a question being asked: does this logic hold? Treating them as conversation starters rather than specs made stakeholder reviews far more productive.
Tight scope forces clarity
Two weeks meant ruthless prioritisation. Focusing only on Rule Management for the POC rather than all five modules kept the concept provable within the timeframe.
POCs are the most honest design work
Without visual polish to hide behind, a POC stands entirely on the strength of its logic and information architecture. It either makes sense or it doesn't, and that clarity is valuable.
